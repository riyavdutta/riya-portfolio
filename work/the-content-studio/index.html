THE IDEA

A studio, not a folder of files.

Every post goes through the same route, and nothing is built until the words are approved.

Campaigns, captions, videos and carousels all live in one place, with finished work kept apart from drafts and named by posting date, so each folder reads in posting order.

THE ROUTE

From brief to posted, in eleven stops.

  1. BriefWho it’s for, what it’s about, the format, the date and what the reader should do.
  2. PlanFor a campaign: theme options, a scored pick, content pillars and a calendar.
  3. CopyDrafted to one story shape: hook, problem, turn, step, lesson.
  4. CheckLine by line: does every line name the exact thing? Anything under 4 out of 5 is rewritten.
  5. ApproveThe gate. Nothing is built until the words are approved.
  6. BuildSlides and scenes are written in code.
  7. RenderVideos with a synthesised soundtrack; carousels as slides plus a PDF.
  8. CheckSample frames from every scene, the duration and the audio.
  9. DeliverDated files land in the finished folders, each with its caption beside it.
  10. WatchEvery video with sound, every slide, every caption read aloud.
  11. PostPaste the caption, upload, post.

Where I sign off.

HOUSE RULES

Every line is checked against these.

Eight house rules sit at the top of the process. Five of them:

  1. 01The visual is the hookOne statement per image; one line of at most eight words per slide or scene.
  2. 02No invented numbersNo statistics, prices or figures that can’t be backed up.
  3. 03No client namesClient work stays out of public posts.
  4. 04The precision testEvery noun has one meaning to a B2B tech founder: not “forecast” but “sales forecast”, not “list” but “CRM contact list”.
  5. 05Pain, not solutionsPosts show the problem and what it costs. The fix is a conversation, not a free how-to.

THE MOTION LAB

A rendering lab for what comes next.

Alongside the studio sits the Motion Lab, a deterministic rendering system. A brief becomes content, content gets treatments, and motion styles, transitions, materials and effects resolve through registries into scenes.

Its catalogue tracks what’s built and what’s planned, and a rule check makes sure every render comes out the same way twice.
