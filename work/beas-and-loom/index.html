Beas & Loom

A concept brand for the Kullu shawl: how to make a real craft’s value visible when the copies look the same.

TYPE
Concept brand. Beas & Loom is not a real company; the craft and the facts are.
CRAFT
The Kullu shawl, Himachal Pradesh (GI No. 19)
MADE
Research brief, positioning, identity and a launch page
MY ROLE
Everything: research, strategy, identity, design and build
THE BRIEF

Make real visible.

A buyer can’t tell a ₹300 power-loom shawl from a handwoven Kullu shawl at a glance, and the GI tag doesn’t help them.

I set myself one brief: rebuild a heritage craft from home as a modern brand, without inventing anything. The craft, the problem and every fact are real; only the label is made up.

What the copies are up against.

Weavers say the GI tag hasn’t stopped the copies. The trusted name, the Bhuttico weavers’ cooperative (1944), sells handwoven shawls at about ₹2,700–3,200, with a cooperative, souvenir feel. Nobody sells the Kullu shawl as a modern heirloom. Sources: The Tribune (an older report), The Tribune, 2009, IP India, Bhuttico.

THREE WAYS IN

How do you make real visible?

A · PROOF OF REAL

Every shawl carries a record: weaver, village, fibre. The brand sells certainty.

B · FREEHAND

Kullu borders are woven freehand, never traced, so each one is a one-off. The brand sells rarity.

C · BOTH (CHOSEN)

Freehand is the story; the record is the proof. One gives the feeling of luxury, the other earns the trust.

THE IDEA

Freehand is the story. The record is the proof.

For people who buy fewer, better things and want to know where they come from: the Kullu shawl made as a modern heirloom, woven by a named weaver and recorded so it can be trusted.

01RealHandwoven on a pit or frame loom in the Kullu valley.
02NamedEvery shawl carries its weaver’s name and village.
03One of oneThe border is woven freehand, and never repeated on purpose.

The proof is physical and simple: a woven mark with the piece number in the selvedge, and a record card that travels with the shawl.

The palette works the way the shawl does: a quiet ground, with all the colour at the edges. Cormorant Garamond for the name, a plain sans for the record, and a woven border band as the one recurring device.

HONEST BY DESIGN

What I wouldn’t make up.

A brand about proving things are real can’t fake its own details.

  1. 01No invented weaversThe example record card leaves the weaver and village blank, and says why.
  2. 02No prices yetPricing should start from a fair weaver wage, which needs real data first.
  3. 03No weaving timeI couldn’t find a reliable source for how long one shawl takes, so the page doesn’t claim one.
  4. 04No borrowed facesNo photos of real artisans without permission; illustration until there are my own.
  5. 05Concept, said plainlyEvery page says Beas & Loom is not a real company.
WHAT’S NEXT

Testing the idea with real buyers.

The positioning rests on one assumption: that people would pay more for a shawl they can trust. Next is a short survey of 10–20 people on how they choose a shawl and what would convince them it’s handwoven, and a source for weaving time so the record can carry it.
